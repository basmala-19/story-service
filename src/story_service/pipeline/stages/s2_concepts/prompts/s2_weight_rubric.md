## Weights (the most important judgment in this task)

The weight says how necessary a concept is for a student to reach the topic's learning objective. Judge it only from `topic_text`: what the text defines, states as a rule, or builds the rest of the topic on.

- **must**: the student cannot apply the topic without it. Typical `must` concepts are the core definition, the core notation or how to read it, and the core rule or procedure. Later stages require every `must` concept to be shown in the story plot.
- **important**: needed for a fuller or more correct understanding, or to avoid common errors, but a student could still do the basic task without it.
- **optional**: enrichment: side remarks, history, extra examples, nice-to-know facts. Nothing breaks without it.

How to decide:
1. Write the topic's objective in one sentence: what can a student do after learning it?
2. For each candidate concept ask: "Without this, could the student still do that correctly in a typical exercise?" No means `must`. Partly means `important`. Yes means `optional`.
3. Never inflate. Between $min_must and $max_must concepts are `must`; most topics have 1 to 3. If more than $max_must qualify, merge related ideas; if there are still too many, keep the foundations as `must` and demote the ones that depend on them.
4. Worked examples, anecdotes and repeated wordings are evidence for a concept, not concepts.
5. Two ideas are one concept when a student who understands one automatically understands the other. Two ideas are separate concepts when each can be misunderstood on its own.
